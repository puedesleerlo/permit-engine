Permit Engine

Decisions you can audit and correct. AI checks the facts, written rules decide, and people settle the hard cases.

Alejandro Tabares, AIM, Heinz College, Carnegie Mellon University

Four parts, each with one job

AI reads and checks. Written rules decide. People settle anything uncertain, and every step is logged.

  1. Check the facts

    AI works out exactly what each claim refers to (which park, which date, which group) and whether the evidence backs it. It never decides.

  2. Assess the risk

    A decision tree measures how likely each problem is and how much harm it would do, like a formal risk assessment.

  3. Apply the rules

    The permit policy, written as code, approves, asks the applicant for missing proof, or proposes a denial that an officer must sign.

  4. People decide the hard cases

    At launch, an officer checks every decision. Once a type of decision passes its tests, officers decide uncertain cases, sign every denial and check 1 in 10 automatic approvals.

Three roles keep people in charge

Permit officers decide cases. A policy owner (the person who signs off on the permit rules) is the only one who changes rules. A knowledge steward (the person who keeps the city’s terms, places and types of proof up to date) corrects facts.

What I built (individual project)

A generator for 240 test requests with known answers, the staged decision system, a learner that tunes how cautious it is, and a test harness that compares it with one AI model. Results pending: see how it will be tested.

The problem

Public offices and regulated firms want AI to help with decisions, but a single AI model that reads an application and answers yes or no has three weaknesses.

  • It can’t show its reasoning

    A single AI model gives an answer, not the facts and rules behind it.

    Here, every decision is logged with its evidence, scores and rules.

  • Tone can sway it

    A pushy letter, or one that drops the mayor’s name, can win a yes that a plain letter would not.

    Here, the same request written four ways should get the same decision.

  • Its mistakes are hard to fix

    When it gets a case wrong, there is no clear part to correct.

    Here, officers’ rulings tune the system, and every change is tested and can be undone.

A sample case, step by step

One permit request, from application to decision. It is an illustrative example, used across this site.

The 5K run in Lincoln Park

The request
A running charity asks the city events office for a 5K run in Lincoln Park: 400 runners, 2 streets closed.
The fact check
The park and date are confirmed. There is no proof that a police escort is arranged.
The risk
Medium: more than 300 people, roads closed, and the police escort is not confirmed.
The decision
On hold: proof requested The city asks once for the police escort letter. If there is no reply, an officer decides.
