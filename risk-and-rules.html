Risk and rules

Risk comes from small trees that a person can read. A decision table written in Rego makes the call, and sends anything it will not decide alone to an officer.

Basic checks come first

Is the fee paid? Is the form complete? Is the date in the permitted window? These run in Rego. If one fails, the case stops and no model is called.

No model involved

Two small trees per hazard

There is one event type, a street race, with three hazards. Each hazard has a likelihood tree and a severity tree. Each tree asks two yes-or-no questions, so it has four leaves.

The three hazards and the questions in their trees
Hazard, and when it appliesTree questions
Traffic conflictA road closesLikelihood: Police escort confirmed (evidence). Traffic plan adequate (Jev).Severity: Arterial road (rule). Crowd over 800 (rule).
Crowd pressureCrowd of 300 or moreLikelihood: Crowd plan adequate (Jev). Crowd over 800 (rule).Severity: Venue too confined (Jev). Crowd over 800 (rule).
Medical emergencyAlways, for a raceLikelihood: Medical plan adequate (Jev). Hot date (rule).Severity: Ambulance on site (evidence). Hospital far (rule).

Three kinds of answer. A rule question is answered by Rego from the form, so it is a plain yes or no. An evidence question is answered by the LLM’s verdict, through a table the steward owns: supported 0.9, unsupported 0.1, contradicted 0.05. A Jev question returns a probability.

A case can go down both branches. A probability sends part of the case each way. A case reaches each leaf with the product of the answers along its path, and the tree’s output is the leaf weights mixed by those amounts.

A leaf holds three weights, one per level. These weights are what training changes. See how they learn.

How risk is summed

For each hazard that applies, likelihood times severity. Then add those up.

Risk
risk = sum of (likelihood × severity)
Likelihood levels stand for
Rare 0.02, possible 0.1, likely 0.3
Severity levels stand for
Minor 0.1, serious 0.4, critical 1
Severity ceiling
0.85. A case whose highest severity reaches it always goes to an officer, because the product can hide a rare catastrophe.

These numbers are policy values, not measurements. The policy owner sets them, each with limits and a reason, in a settings file. A value outside its limits stops the run.

Thresholds are chosen, not set by hand. The lower one is the highest value where cases below it are at most 5% labelled risky. The upper one is the lowest value where cases at or above it are at least 85% labelled risky. If they cross, both take their midpoint. If no value meets a target, nothing is approved automatically, or nothing is marked high risk, and the report says so.

These targets tend to fall short on new cases, and the sealed test is too small to confirm them. See what the test can and cannot show.

The decision table

First match wins. It is written in Rego and run through Open Policy Agent, as a pure function of its input. Each result gives a row, a route, a reason and the clauses behind it.

The decision table, checked from top to bottom
RowIf this is trueThen
0A basic check failed: fee, form or dateReturned or denialAn incomplete form is returned. Otherwise a denial is proposed, and an officer signs it
1The system will not decide alone: evidence contradicted, a requirement unclear, documents that fit no kind, a model or Rego failed, the stop switch on, or the highest severity at the ceilingOfficer decides
2A requirement failedDenial proposedA denial is proposed, citing the clause. An officer signs it
3A requirement is missingOfficer decides
4Risk is under the lower thresholdApproved
5Risk is at or over the upper thresholdOfficer decides
6Anything else: risk is inside the review bandOfficer decides

A missing requirement goes to an officer. Nothing writes to the applicant.

Open evidence stays soft. A case with no escort letter can still be approved at row 4, if its risk is low enough. The evidence moves the risk, and only a contradiction stops the case.

What an officer can do: approve, which issues the permit; sign a proposed denial; reject a proposed denial and approve instead; or deny directly. A permit is issued only with a logged decision or ruling as its parent event.

The stop switch

A setting the policy owner controls. With it on, every case that passes basic checks goes to an officer, and nothing is approved automatically until it is turned off. A test covers this. It is off in the demo’s settings.
